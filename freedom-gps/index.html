The economy, in plain terms

Three numbers that actually change what your debt costs you — and what paying it off earns you. Checked Nov 4, 2026.

3.75–4.00%
Held steady Oct 28, 2026 · next decision Dec 9

This is the rate underneath almost every card and loan you hold. When it stays up, your APRs stay up with it — and every extra dollar you put on high-rate debt earns you a guaranteed return at that debt’s rate. No savings account pays that.

Source: Federal Reserve, FOMC statement, Oct 28, 2026.

3.7%
Prices vs. a year ago · September 2026

Prices rose 3.7% over the past year. Any debt charging more than that is costing you real money above and beyond rising prices — and the faster you clear it, the less of that gap you pay.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, CPI release, Oct 14, 2026.

48.0
University of Michigan index · October 2026

Shoppers are feeling the squeeze — this reading sits near the lowest ever recorded, and households expect prices to rise about 4.7% over the next year. You’re not imagining it: money feels tighter because it is. A written route is how you take control back.

Source: University of Michigan, Surveys of Consumers, Oct 23, 2026.
